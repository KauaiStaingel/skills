# Calibration and direction

Calibration converts product strategy into a visual and interaction commitment before implementation makes the first plausible idea expensive to discard.

## Commit to words

Choose 3–5 precise adjectives grounded in the product. Prefer informative tensions:

- precise, not clinical;
- energetic, not childish;
- premium, not exclusive;
- dense, not cluttered;
- friendly, not casual.

Avoid empty directions such as “modern,” “clean,” “beautiful,” or “professional” unless qualified by observable characteristics.

## Classify the page intention

Name what the person is doing on this surface. The product category alone is insufficient: the same software can have a persuasive landing page, a reading-oriented documentation page, and an operational dashboard.

| Intention | Primary behavior | Typical priority |
|---|---|---|
| Convince | Understand an offer and decide or act | Concrete proposition, real evidence, clear action |
| Operate | Complete recurring tasks | Familiar patterns, efficiency, state clarity, density |
| Read | Learn, compare, or consult | Measure, hierarchy, navigation, durable links |
| Show | Experience work, media, or a launch | The work leads; interface recedes or choreographs |

Use this label as a starting point, not a genre template.

## Declare three observable axes

Calibrate the selected direction so later review is more objective:

| Axis | Low | Medium | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Variation | Repeated alignment, calm or single-column composition | Stable grid with one or two intentional breaks | Composition frequently changes scale, overlap, bleed, or alignment |
| Motion | State feedback only | One restrained reveal or transition per meaningful region | One choreographed signature interaction, with quiet support |
| Density | One idea or task per viewport region | Several related blocks with moderate spacing | Compact controls, tables, or multi-column information for expert work |

High does not mean “more effects,” and low does not mean “unfinished.” Density is not clutter; variation is not randomness; motion is not decoration. State the chosen levels and verify the render against them.

## Derive from the subject

Look for visual and structural material in the product's world:

- domain artifacts, notation, tools, documents, environments, and workflows;
- audience expectations and level of expertise;
- physical materials, geography, era, or cultural context when relevant;
- characteristic data shapes and actions;
- brand history and existing distribution surfaces.

Transpose principles from references. Do not copy their layout, content, or brand expression.

## Generate real alternatives

When meaningful freedom exists, create two or three candidates that differ structurally, not just by accent color. Vary axes such as:

- information density and reveal strategy;
- editorial versus tool-like composition;
- grid behavior and alignment;
- typography role and scale contrast;
- image-led, data-led, task-led, or content-led opening;
- surface and elevation model;
- motion character.

Compare each candidate using:

1. Product and audience fit.
2. Clarity of the primary task.
3. Accessibility and platform familiarity.
4. Differentiation from category defaults.
5. Feasibility in the existing stack and scope.
6. Durability across screens and states.

For an ongoing or multi-screen product, place durable decisions in the established design document or `DESIGN.md`. For a small change, keep the plan in working notes or the handoff.

## Signature move

Use one memorable move that follows from the brief: a particular data representation, composition, crop system, typographic relationship, interaction, or graphic device. It must survive removal of decorative effects and remain compatible with the task.

Do not make every element distinctive. Spend boldness once and use restraint around it.

## Default review

Before committing, ask:

- Would this direction plausibly be produced for any product in this category?
- Does the bare block structure already resemble a common template?
- Did a component library choose the visual identity by accident?
- Does every color, type role, container, and effect have a job?
- Is the direction only a reaction to last year's cliché, forming a newer cliché?

If yes, revisit the product's subject matter and structure rather than adding more decoration.
