# Distinctiveness without generated-UI defaults

The goal is not novelty for its own sake. Replace statistical defaults with decisions that trace back to the product.

## The convergence mechanism

Generic output usually comes from:

1. Missing constraints, causing safe average choices.
2. Designing before understanding the artifact.
3. Letting a starter library determine composition and brand.
4. Using a checklist of current trends instead of a coherent direction.
5. Polishing the first plausible structure instead of comparing alternatives.

Address the mechanism rather than freezing a permanent blacklist.

## Common first-order tells

These are warnings only when unjustified or accumulated:

- purple/indigo gradients as automatic brand expression;
- centered hero, generic badge, broad claim, and two CTAs;
- three equal feature cards with icon tiles;
- untouched starter-kit typography, radii, shadows, and iconography;
- gradient text, glass panels, glowing blobs, and decorative grids;
- identical rounded containers for every content type;
- stock or abstract imagery where product evidence belongs;
- vague copy promising transformation without concrete meaning.

## Common second-order tells

Avoid escaping one median by adopting another automatically:

- cream, high-contrast serif, and terracotta;
- near-black with acid green or orange;
- editorial hairlines and zero-radius grids unrelated to the content;
- all-caps mono labels and decorative technical metadata;
- one accented or italic word in every headline;
- decorative `01 / 02 / 03` where content is not sequential;
- a one-hue palette with no functional color roles;
- forced brutalism, asymmetry, texture, or maximalism used only to signal originality.

Every one of these can be correct for the right brief. The failure is using it because the axis was left open.

## Product-specific sources of identity

Prefer:

- domain-specific content and terminology;
- characteristic data shapes and workflows;
- real product visuals, artifacts, or environments;
- composition derived from the primary job;
- typography chosen for language, audience, and tone;
- one recognizable interaction or graphic device;
- details that remain coherent across empty, error, loading, and mobile states.

## Anti-convergence checks

- **Bare structure:** Remove color, shadows, and imagery. Does the layout still express the product or only a template?
- **Silhouette:** Reduce the page to large black and white blocks. Could it belong unchanged to an unrelated business?
- **Swap test:** Replace the brand name and copy. If nothing else feels wrong, the design may be insufficiently grounded.
- **Default stack:** Count categories of unmodified defaults. Several weak signals together matter more than one familiar choice.
- **Reference trace:** Can each borrowed principle be explained without naming the source site?
- **Signature survival:** Does the signature move still work without decorative effects?

Do not sacrifice clarity, accessibility, familiar behavior, or product continuity merely to pass these checks.
