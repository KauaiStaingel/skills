# Optional motion and composition patterns

Read this only when the calibrated direction calls for medium or high variation or motion. Operational and reading surfaces usually need few of these patterns.

## Rule of one strong move

Choose one signature treatment and two to four quiet supporting behaviors. Multiple strong effects competing in one viewport produce a demo reel, not a coherent interface. Every moving experience needs a useful static and reduced-motion state.

## Reading-order entrance

Reveal a small opening group in reading order—such as title, supporting text, and action—with short opacity and vertical-transform changes. Keep the primary action available quickly.

It becomes a tell when every region repeats the same entrance, the delay blocks use, or an otherwise unnecessary eyebrow is added to make the sequence look richer.

## One-time regional reveal

Reveal one meaningful below-the-fold group when it enters the viewport. Limit stagger to a few related items and run it once.

It becomes a tell when paragraphs, list rows, footer items, and every card wait for animation before becoming readable.

## Anchored media composition

Place copy where the real subject leaves usable visual space, adjusting crop and `object-position` before covering faces or shrinking type. A real subject may deliberately cross a frame edge when prepared as a layered asset.

It becomes a tell when the subject is a generic floating object, chrome mascot, synthetic statue, or arbitrary 3D blob.

## Scroll-linked statement

A single short statement can change emphasis as the user scrolls when the reading rhythm is the experience. Keep the initial state readable and offer a static reduced-motion alternative.

It becomes a tell when applied to body copy, repeated in multiple sections, or used to compensate for weak content.

## Sticky sequence

A short set of truly sequential cases or steps may stack or remain sticky to preserve context while the next item enters.

It becomes a tell when unrelated features are forced into a sequence or the behavior traps scrolling, focus, or touch use.

## Media transition under stable type

Keep a stable textual anchor while real media changes through a restrained cut or crossfade. Reserve dimensions and make the first useful state available without scripting.

It becomes a tell when the media is decorative, loops attention indefinitely, or the transition exists only to signal “premium.”

## Interactive label motion

A button label may shift or roll on hover for one high-character action when the duplicate is hidden from assistive technology and reduced motion falls back to a color or underline change.

It becomes a tell when every link performs it or when motion obscures the action label.

## Avoid as defaults

- blur-based entrances and animated gradients;
- typewriter, decode, or blinking-cursor effects on static content;
- bounce, elastic, or overshoot motion in routine controls;
- infinite marquees, status pulses, clocks, weather chips, and scroll cues without product meaning;
- animated counters without verified data and context;
- parallax applied to a flat stock image;
- motion on noninteractive cards that implies a missing action.

Prefer opacity and transform for ordinary UI animation, keep timings proportional to the task, and validate on low-power devices when motion is material.
